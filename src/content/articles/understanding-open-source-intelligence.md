> **Sample article.** The examples below are illustrative and make no claims about the site owner’s work.

Open-source intelligence is less about finding one decisive clue than building a defensible chain of observations. Collection is only the beginning; source evaluation and uncertainty management determine whether the result is useful.

## Collection is not conclusion

A public post, repository commit, archived page, or satellite image is an observation. Treating it as a conclusion skips the analytical work between evidence and claim.

## Keep an evidence ledger

For each source, record the retrieval time, original location, preservation method, and the claim it is intended to support. This makes disagreement inspectable rather than rhetorical.

## State uncertainty

Use language proportional to the evidence. Distinguish what is directly observed, what is inferred, and what remains unknown. A concise uncertainty statement often adds more value than another page of collected material.
